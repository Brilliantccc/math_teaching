# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Teachers and middle/high school students in a Chinese educational context. Teachers manage question banks, assemble tests, and track student progress. Students practice problems, review wrong answers, and monitor their learning stats. Usage happens both at-desk (teachers preparing exams) and on-mobile (students practicing after school).

## Product Purpose

A math question bank management system that makes it easy for teachers to organize, categorize, and deploy math questions, and for students to practice, learn from mistakes, and track their progress. Success means teachers spend less time on logistics and more on teaching, while students build confidence through structured practice and visible progress.

## Brand Personality

Friendly, encouraging, approachable. Like a helpful tutor rather than a cold database. Three words: **warm, clear, motivating**. The interface should feel welcoming to students who might be anxious about math, while remaining efficient enough for teachers who need to work fast.

## Anti-references

- Dense, chaotic data UIs with overwhelming tables and no visual hierarchy
- Generic corporate SaaS dashboards with soulless Bootstrap templates
- Dated gradients, beveled buttons, cluttered "Web 2.0" aesthetics
- Cold, clinical interfaces that feel like database admin tools

## Design Principles

1. **Encouragement over anxiety** — Math can be intimidating. The interface should feel supportive, using warm colors, friendly copy, and visible progress to build confidence rather than pressure.
2. **Clarity through hierarchy** — Every screen has a clear primary action. Information is layered: summary first, details on demand. No screen should feel cluttered.
3. **Efficient for power users, gentle for newcomers** — Teachers need speed and density when managing questions; students need simplicity and guidance when practicing. The same system serves both without compromising either.
4. **Progress is visible** — Stats, streaks, mastery markers — make learning progress tangible and motivating. Students should feel a sense of accomplishment.
5. **Respect the math** — Mathematical content (formulas, equations) must render beautifully and be easy to read. The UI never competes with the content.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance. Good contrast ratios (≥4.5:1 for body text). Keyboard navigation support. Semantic HTML. The existing dark/light theme system addresses reduced-motion and contrast preferences. Consideration for students with color vision deficiencies in status indicators (use icons + color, not color alone).
